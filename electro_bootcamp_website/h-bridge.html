Make a motor go both ways

A battery alone only pushes current one way. Flip the battery and the motor flips too. An H-bridge is a clever switch puzzle that can flip that path without ever unplugging the battery.

2.1 Battery + motor

Power a motor with a battery only

Remember voltage and current from Circuits? Voltage is the push. Current is the flow. A lamp lights when the battery sits across it. A motor is the same idea — except the flow turns into spin.

The battery only pushes one way: out of + and back into −. So the motor only spins one way. Rotate the battery and the terminals swap. The push flips. Current goes the other way. The motor reverses.

This is why we need an H-bridge next. On a race car you cannot flip the battery every time you want to reverse.

Battery across a motor

The battery pushes charges out of +. That flow is current, and it spins the motor one way.

2.2 What is an H-bridge?

Four switches, one motor, letter H

You just flipped the battery by hand. On a car that is messy. An H-bridge does the same job with switches. Imagine the motor sitting on the cross-bar of a letter H. Opposite corners on = spin. Same side on = danger.

H-bridge switch view

Tap a switch button, or use IN1 / IN2 to set a whole side at once.

IN1 and IN2

IN1 controls the left leg

High → S1 ON, S2 OFF

Low → S1 OFF, S2 ON

IN2 controls the right leg

High → S3 ON, S4 OFF

Low → S3 OFF, S4 ON

Coast / open
IN1IN2Motor action
LowLowBrake to − · S2 + S4
HighLowForward · S1 + S4
LowHighReverse · S2 + S3
HighHighBrake to + · S1 + S3

Tap switches one by one to experiment. If S1 and S2 are both ON — or S3 and S4 — current skips the motor and the battery and wire burn.

2.3 Assemble on a breadboard

Build the H, then poke the switches

Your challenge: copy the H-bridge onto a breadboard. Then change which switches are closed and watch the motor. Predict first. Observe second.

  1. Place the motor as the cross-bar.
  2. Add four switch paths to VCC and GND.
  3. Close S1 + S4. Note the spin.
  4. Close S2 + S3. The spin should flip.
  5. Do not close both switches on the same side.

Observation log

Write what you saw on paper, then lock it in with the quiz below.

S1 + S4

Current should cross the motor one way. Label that “forward” on your chassis.

S2 + S3

Current crosses the other way. That is reverse, even if the wheel looks “wrong” until you define forward.

If nothing spins: check battery, motor wires, and that opposite corners — not the same pillar — are closed.

2.4 Connection

PCB to motor and power

The provided PCB is the solidified H-bridge. You no longer jumper every switch by hand. You do still have to join motor and battery the right way.

1/4

Find the motor outputs

On your provided PCB, locate the four motor outputs. These come from the H-bridge. The text below the screw hole describes which terminal and side of motor to connect. RM- stands for Right Motor −, likewise LM+ stands for Left Motor +.
