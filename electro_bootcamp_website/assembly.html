Section 4.0 · Afternoon

Build your car. Make it yours.

About 28 kits look identical until you mark them. Fit the motors, seat the PCB, keep the wires honest, then prove it in the enclosed test zone — not the hallway.

4.1 Chassis assembly

From board to vehicle

1/5

Fit the motors

Clip or screw both motors onto the chassis so the wheels sit straight.

Crooked motors make the car drift.

Step 1 photo

Drop step 1 photo: motors on the chassis

Do not mix cars

Customise the chassis

Tape, marker, sticker, or a name plate — anything visible from two metres away. If two cars look the same, someone will pick up the wrong remote pair after a crash.

Name it

Write your name under the chassis and on the top shell.

Colour code

Match a tape colour on the car to a note on your phone’s Wi-Fi name.

Keep it light

Decorate, but do not bury the antenna or block the wheels.

Test-run rules

  • Drive only in the enclosed area the facilitators mark.
  • No throwing, dropping, or “just to see if it survives”.
  • If it smells hot or a wheel jams, power off and raise a hand.
  • Extra kits and batteries are ready — tell us before something dies mid-solder of your patience.

4.2 Test run

Prove it works, then stop

A test run is not the Grand Prix. You are checking: wheels on, Wi-Fi still linked, forward really means forward, and nothing is scraping the floor.

Reminder: you take this car home. Treat the test zone like a lab, not a demolition derby.
